# Phase 2: free geometry (convex pieces at any angle, with beam-theory joints)

**Goal.** A building can be made of convex pieces at any angle, not only boxes on the 0.125 m grid: diagonal braces and rafters, sloped slabs and roofs, rotated wings, arches and vaults of wedge stones, curved facades of segments. They stand, carry load and break by the same structural model as the boxes, at about the same cost per step.

**Gate.** A complex building (irregular plan, double-height spaces, a braced steel frame, a sloped roof on rafters, an arcade of masonry arches, room contents) stands under its own weight, collapses gradually when cut, and passes the lab's checks, with the engine tests below matching closed-form results.

## What already exists

The structure is already a rigid-body-spring network (docs/STRUCTURE.md): every cell is a rigid body with 6 unknowns, every face a spring with normal, shear, bending and torsion stiffness from the materials and the face's section, solved by preconditioned conjugate gradients with a multigrid. Joints of convex cells are polygons with their own frames (`geo_of`), and the springs work in that frame. So beam theory at the joints is there; what is missing is geometry:
- every brick is an axis-aligned box on the tick grid (`x y z`, `sx sy sz`), or one of the hull library's 63 fixed cells mapped into such a box;
- joints are found as shared box faces on the grid (`jaxis` 0-2), or as faces of one fractured brick's cells (`jaxis` 3);
- cracks cut bricks along grid planes;
- the GPU knows the boxes and the library's hulls only.

## Steps

Each step ends with the checks in CLAUDE.md and a note in this file.

1. **Free pieces in the store.** A brick may be a free convex piece: up to 16 corners and 12 faces (the GPU's hull limits), a centre on a 1 mm grid and a rotation. Free pieces keep their data in a side table, so box bricks keep their 25 B. Volume, centroid and inertia come from the polytope. Engine tests: mass properties of a rotated box, a wedge, a prism.
   *Done (9 November, branch phase-2).* `Bricks::add_piece(centre, rotation, points, ...)` makes the hull of the points (`hull::convex`: the planes through three points with every point on one side, then the polytope of those planes), refused if flat, beyond the GPU's hull limits or longer than a brick's box (255 ticks, 31.9 m). The brick's `hull` is `HULL_FREE` (255) and the piece (`Piece`: centre in mm, rotation as an f32 quaternion, corners in its own frame as f32, face loops, volume and centroid) sits in `Bricks::pieces`, found by `piece_of`; its box is the tick box that holds its corners, so the spatial index and queries work on it unchanged. `hull::moments` gives volume, centroid and inertia tensor of any convex polytope (tetrahedra from the corners' mean, second moments V/20 (sum x_i x_j + S_i S_j)); `Bricks::shape`, `volume`, `centroid` and `inertia` work for boxes, cells and pieces alike. The engine mesh draws a piece's faces (not one lying on the ground). Save and load carry the pieces.
   Engine tests (`brick::piece_tests`, `cargo test --release`): a 2 x 1 x 0.5 m box turned 0.7 rad about (1, 2, 3) has volume 1 m^3 and inertia R diag(1.25, 4.25, 5)/12 R^T within 1e-6; a right wedge (legs 1.2 and 0.6 m, 2 m long) has V = abL/2, centroid (a/3, b/3, L/2), Ixy = L a^2 b^2/72 and the closed-form diagonal, and the same turned 90 degrees; a hexagonal prism (side 0.4 m, 1.5 m) has V = 3 sqrt 3/2 s^2 H and Iyy = 5 V s^2/12; a 20-sided prism, flat points and a 40 m piece are refused; a cube turned 45 degrees on a cube joins nothing by its box (0 joints), its mesh is six outward faces enclosing 1 m^3, and it survives a save and load.
   Not yet: a free piece joins nothing (step 2), the page and the GPU do not get them (step 5), and no building makes them, so nothing that runs changes.
   *Shapes kept once (9 November, with step 7).* A `Piece` is its centre, rotation and shape number; the shapes (`PieceShape`: corners, faces, volume, centroid) are in `Bricks::shapes`, each kept once (`intern`, keyed by its corners to 10 um and its faces), so identical members share one; `Bricks::piece(i)` gives a `PieceRef` with both. The complex building's 302 pieces have 78 shapes, and the store went from 370 to 186 B a brick.

2. **Joints between any pieces.** Two faces join where they are coplanar and opposed (within 1 degree and 5 mm) and their polygons overlap: the joint is the overlap polygon, with its normal and frame. Box to box stays on the fast grid path. Engine tests: a rotated box on a box, wedge to wedge, a brace end on a column face; areas against hand results.
   *Done (9 November).* A joint with a free piece has `jaxis` `JFREE` (4). `Bricks::free_face(p, q)` takes the faces of p and q (boxes, cells and pieces alike, from `shape`) that face each other within 1 degree (`JOIN_COS`) with planes within 5 mm (`JOIN_GAP`, centroid of q's face from p's plane), projects q's face onto p's plane and cuts it to p's face: the largest such overlap of at least 1 cm^2 (`JOIN_MIN_AREA`) is the joint, its normal from ja to jb. `contact`, `build_joints`, `split` (a box joined to a piece hands the joint on) and `joint_poly` use it; box to box stays on the grid path. In the structure solver a free piece is one node; a JFREE joint becomes one link per cell of a box it bears on (the polygon cut to each cell's column), in the polygon's own frame, and the joint is checked as one section summed about its centre.
   Engine tests (`brick::piece_tests`): a cube turned 45 degrees on a cube joins on the octagon 2 (sqrt 2 - 1) = 0.8284 m^2 (before: no joint; its box would have given 1 m^2), normal up; a cube turned 30 degrees on a 2 m block joins on its whole 1 m^2; two wedges of a cut cube join on the diagonal, sqrt 2 m^2, normal (1, -1, 0)/sqrt 2; a 0.2 m brace at 45 degrees joins a column face on 0.04 / cos 45 = 0.05657 m^2, normal +x; tilted 0.5 degrees a block joins and 1.5 degrees not, lifted 4 mm joins and 6 mm not; a turned cube with 3 MN on it, centred over the line between two 2 m cells of a slab, is checked as pure compression: ratio 0.10078 against (W + 3 MN) / 1 m^2 / 30 MPa = 0.10078.

3. **The structure solver over free pieces.** A long free piece (a 6 m brace) is cut at build time into segments of at most 2 m by planes across its length, so it bends as the boxes' cells do. Links come from the joint polygons. Engine tests against closed-form results:
   - an inclined strut carries P / sin(theta);
   - a braced portal frame under a sideways load is stiffer than the unbraced one, by the ratio of the closed-form frame;
   - a three-hinged arch of wedge stones under a uniform load pushes out at its feet with H = w L^2 / 8 f;
   - a sloped rafter roof pushes out on its walls (no tie) and does not with a tie.
   *Done (9 November).* `Bricks::cut_piece(i, n, d)` cuts a free piece by a plane into two convex pieces (its corners on each side and where its edges cross), hands its joints to the part(s) that still meet each neighbour and joins the two on the cut; `Bricks::segment(i, 2.0)` cuts a long piece across its length (its longest extent along one of its edges) into equal segments. `Analysis::forces` gives every joint's force and moment in its face's frame, for the tests. (Moving a piece's box in the spatial index without unlinking it first looped the index's lists: the A-frame's `build_joints` ran out of memory; `set_box` unlinks first now, and every test that cuts a piece goes through it.)
   Engine tests (`brick::piece_tests`):
   - a 3 m bar turned 20 degrees cut through its middle: two halves of 0.135 m^3 joined on 0.09 m^2; a 7 m bar segmented: four 1.75 m pieces in a row, three joints;
   - an A-frame of 0.2 m steel struts, 4 m long (3 segments each), 100 kN on its head: at 45 degrees each strut carries 70.40 kN (P / 2 sin theta = 70.71) with 0.31 kN of shear across it, its foot pushes down 50.0 kN and out 49.6 kN (P / 2 tan theta = 50.0); at 60 degrees 57.65 kN (57.74), 50.0 and 28.7 kN (28.9). The rest is frame action of the moment-resisting joints;
   - a parabolic arch of 12 wedge stones, span 6 m, rise 1.5 m, 50 kN/m: crown thrust 143.8, 148.4 and 149.6 kN at 0.4, 0.2 and 0.1 m thick against w L^2 / 8 f = 150.0, no shear at the crown. The shortfall is the stones' elastic shortening (a hingeless arch thrusts H0 / (1 + c I / (A f^2)); c = 45/4 fixed, 15/8 two-hinged, about 7 here): it quarters at each halving of the thickness, and extrapolated to no thickness the thrust is 149.9 kN. (The plan said a three-hinged arch: the engine has no hinge to put at the crown and feet of a stone arch, so the test checks the elastic arch's limit instead.)
   - a braced portal frame (`a_braced_portal_frame_is_stiffer_by_its_brace`): 0.25 m concrete columns 3 m tall, 4 m apart, 10 kN sideways. Unbraced, with the columns cut into 12 bricks, it sways 1.92 mm against 1.96 mm by the closed form for a fixed-foot portal (k = 24 E Ic / h^3 (1 + 6 rho) / (4 + 6 rho), h to the footings' and beam's centres). Braced by a 0.15 m concrete brace at 40 degrees (3 segments), the brace takes 0.9449 of the load against k_b / (k_b + k_f) = 0.9450, and the frame is 12.5 times as stiff. Not 18 = (k_b + k_f) / k_f: the corners where the brace meets the frame give, while the brace itself stretches P l / EA per segment (0.0219 mm per 1.48 m segment, against 0.0219). Found on the way: a column of one brick sways 2.2 times too much (docs/STRUCTURE.md, Known limits);
   - a rafter roof on piers (`a_rafter_roof_pushes_its_walls_out_unless_tied`): 45-degree steel rafters on 3 m concrete piers, 100 kN at the ridge (thrust H = 50 kN). Untied, they push the pier tops out 29.0 kN (the piers lean and the rigid-jointed frame spreads and bends) and overload the piers (worst ratio 1.34). With a tie 0.5, 0.3, 0.15 m above the feet the push is 8.9, 5.6, 3.8 kN; at 0.15 m the tie carries 46.0 kN (tie and push add up to H), the rafters 70.56 kN axially (P / 2 sin 45 = 70.71) and the worst ratio is 0.19. On rigid feet the tie carries almost nothing (1.2 kN): feet that cannot spread need none.
   The springs of a free piece: where a free piece meets a face at an angle (a brace's end cut level), its half of the link is a bar from its centroid to the face, stretched along that line and sheared across it, over its true section A |n . r|; the halves' compliances add as tensors and the spring's translation works in that sum's own frame (`eig3`), its rotation in the face's (`Spring::frr`). Boxes and cells keep the rule they had (the normal's distance and the face's area), so the box buildings do not change.

4. **Breaking free pieces.** A crack cuts a piece by the plane of the failing section into two convex pieces, joints clipped exactly. Crushing and spalling as for boxes. Engine tests: a brace snaps where its moment peaks; an arch fails by four hinges when one foot moves out.
   *Engine part done (9 November).* A free piece breaks where the boxes do: at its joints, which for a long piece are its segments' joints at most 2 m apart (the boxes' cells), so the failing section is a joint and the existing failure path removes or cracks it. `Bricks::cut(i, axis, at)` cuts any brick at an axis plane: boxes (and cells) `split` as before, a free piece by the plane itself (`cut_piece`); the section cutter of impacts (`sim.rs`, `cut_section`) uses it. A free piece on a fracture surface shatters (`shatter_piece`: two cuts near its centroid, the first across its longest extent, at angles and offsets from a hash of the brick as the boxes' patterns), into up to four convex parts of which one or two spall off. `hull::convex` now builds its faces from the points themselves (planes through three points with all on one side within 1 um per m; corners in order by a 2D hull): through plane intersections at 1e-9, corners kept in f32 missed faces and a piece cut four ways came out 9% too big (0.782 m^3 against 0.72).
   Engine tests (`brick::piece_tests`):
   - an arch loaded off centre forms four hinges: the 0.4 m arch above with 150 kN more on its fourth stone; the line of thrust's eccentricity e = M / N swings -0.081 m at the left springing, +0.215 m under the load (past the 0.2 m half-thickness), -0.097 m at the far haunch, +0.051 m at the right springing, the four-hinge mechanism's pattern; the joints over strength are 3, 4 (under the load) and 8, 9 (far haunch); at 50 kN none. (The plan said "when one foot moves out": the analysis applies loads, not movements, so the test loads it off centre, which forms the same mechanism.)
   - a beam of free pieces breaks where its moment peaks: an 8 m beam turned 30 degrees in plan, 4 segments, clamped on two blocks, 60 kN on each middle segment: ratios 0.32, 1.36, 0.32 at 2, 4, 6 m; of its own joints only the mid-span one is over strength;
   - a free piece cut by an axis plane halves (0.72 m^3 each; a plane past it leaves it whole) and its upper half shatters into four parts that add up to its volume, joined to each other and to the lower half, one or two spalling.
   Crushing a free piece in an impact goes through `cut` (the plane cuts it as it cuts boxes). Still to do with step 5: an impact test on a falling free piece, which needs the page and the GPU to have free pieces.

5. **To the page and the GPU.** Free pieces go out as hulls: a runtime hull table on the GPU (the library's slots, then pieces uploaded as they are made, same limits), drawn and collided like the chunks. Meshes: each face minus what touching pieces cover. GPU tests: a rotated hull rests flat on a box; a wedge stack stands.
   *Done (9 November).* GPU: runtime hulls after the library's 63 (`HULL_RUNTIME`, 4096 slots in the shape buffer), each in its own box mapped to [-1,1]^3 like the library's cells, so a free piece is a hull shape placed by its p, q and stretched by its h. `GpuWorld.pieceShape(corners, faces, centre, rotation)` makes the hull (`hullOfCorners`: planes, segments, directions, volume, centroid and covariance, the GPU's limits), registers it by content (`registerHull`: the same corners and faces take the same slot; slots are not freed) and gives the shape; a world uploads the hulls registered since its last step (`uploadHulls`). `setStaticShape` takes a rotation, so a standing free piece collides as itself. Library and runtime hulls share the packing (`packHull`). Engine to page: a free piece's hull code is 256 + its index in `Bricks::pieces` (the code is a float, exact to 2^24: up to 32,512 pieces), and `piece_shape(k)` puts its centre, rotation, corners and faces into a buffer of its own (`piece_out`, so it can be read while `out` is). The page (`boxShape`, `pieceShapeOf`) turns it into the GPU shape; a falling free piece's rotation composes with its body's; a piece beyond the hull limits or past the runtime slots falls back to its box (counted, `piecesAsBoxes`). A runtime hull's instanced mesh holds 256 instances, not the library's 16,384 (1.2 MB of buffers a mesh).
   GPU test `freehull`: a wedge (legs 1.0 and 0.5 m, 0.6 m long) dropped 0.2 m onto a slab lands flat (down 0.2000 m, turned 0.00 degrees); three courses of two wedges (boxes cut on their diagonal) stand (worst wedge 6.6 mm from where it started); a block on a static slab turned 15 degrees rests on the slope (5.2 mm, 0.00 degrees).

6. **A complex building and its scenarios.** A generator (seeded) for the building in the gate, with room contents as loose bodies. Lab scenarios: cut the ground storey, take out a corner column, blast a room, knock out an arch's keystone; checks for volume, gradual collapse, contents, settling, and the spread over several seeds.
   *Begun (9 November).* Kind 4, `game/src/complex.rs`: a steel frame of two 4 m storeys on a 3 x 2 grid of 6 m bays (0.5 m steel columns, 0.5 m deep beams on the grid lines, 0.25 m concrete floors), the first floor left out over one bay for a double-height hall; eight diagonal steel braces (free pieces, 0.2 m, 4 segments each) in the end bays of the long facades, from a column's foot to the next column's face under the beam; a pitched roof at 26 degrees: a steel ridge beam on four posts, 24 timber rafters (0.1 x 0.25 m, 4 segments) from the roof slab by the eaves to the ridge, 22 roof boards on them (0.05 m, 4 segments); in front, an arcade of seven 0.75 m masonry piers 3 m tall with six semicircular arches of nine stones (ring 0.375 m) and a 0.5 m stone beam on the keystones (12 segments). 363 bricks, 282 of them free pieces, 896 joints, built in 47 ms. Engine test `complex::tests::the_complex_building_is_what_it_should_be_and_stands`: the parts are all there and joined where they should be, nothing detached, and under its own weight nothing fails (worst ratio 0.387). The page offers it as a building (settings) and the lab has a scenario, `complex` (the ground storey and the arcade's piers cut).
   Room contents: two desks, a cabinet and a chair turned 25 degrees in each first-floor bay (20 bricks), loose (`F_LOOSE`: joined to nothing). With both: 398 bricks, 302 free pieces, 924 joints; the 20 contents and nothing else stand loose, and the worst ratio is still 0.387.
   Lab scenarios on it: `complex` (the ground storey and the arcade's piers cut, as the other buildings), `complexcol` (the north-east corner column shot out), `complexroom` (a 1.5 m blast in a first-floor room), `keystone` (the third arch's keystone shot out); suite `complex` (`node game/lab/lab.cjs complex`), all four in `full`.
   Blasts and free pieces: a blast refines what it touches, then crumbles what has its centre inside it; free pieces were not refined, so whole 1.5 m segments went when their centres were inside (parts of them well outside the blast). Now cut down like boxes (`refine_bricks` through `Bricks::cut`): a 1.5 m blast on a brace takes 0.127 m^3 of its 0.238 (0.162 before) and leaves it in more, smaller pieces (`complex::tests::a_blast_takes_free_pieces_apart_as_it_does_boxes`).
   Particles against free pieces: they took every hull for its box, mostly air for an inclined piece built unturned; they now test its faces (docs/GPU.md, Convex chunks; GPU test `freehullp`).

## Limits written down

- A free piece is convex; anything else is several pieces.
- Curves are segments (an arch of 9 to 15 stones, a curved facade of 1-2 m panels).
- Joints form only between faces that meet flat: two pieces touching at an edge or a corner rest on each other through the GPU's contacts once broken loose, but are not joined while standing.
